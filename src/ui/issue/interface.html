THE STORY — EVERYONE AT THE TABLE KNOWS THIS MUCH

There is a blue diamond called La Colombe — the Dove. It is the size of a thumbnail and it has been in the Vernay family for a hundred and forty years. Every August it comes out of the bank in Nice and sits on the dinner table under a little glass dome, because Sacha de Vernay likes his guests to see what money looks like.

Sacha is sixty-two, immensely rich, and famous for the people he collects. This summer he invited seven of them to the villa. He wrote to each of you privately, and each letter said the same strange thing: come on the fifteenth, I have something to announce that concerns you.

You arrived this afternoon. It has rained since five. The coast road is under water and the telephone is dead.

At half past seven the housekeeper went to call Sacha down to dinner and found him on the floor of the study.

The glass dome on the dinner table is empty.

Nobody can leave until the road clears in the morning. So you will have dinner, all seven of you, and by the end of it you will know two things: who killed him, and who took the Dove. They are not the same person.

One file each, drawn at random. Once it is issued it is yours for the evening — and it names nobody but you.
